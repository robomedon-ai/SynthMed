Project Results & Findings

Two synthesis applications — placental MRI (PASD) and cerebrovascular angiography (TopBrain) — with opposite winning architectures. Live numbers below are sourced from REPORT.md and the pasd_eval/ & cv_eval/ JSON reports.

PASD · placental MRI

Cross-modality augmentation wins everywhere

CycleGAN BTFE↔TSE translation produces synthetic pairs that beat every mask-conditional generator at every data size, on both modalities. At N=50 real slices, +xmod adds +7.3 pp Dice; even at full data it's the only augmentation that doesn't hurt.

+7.3 pp at N=50 +2.3 pp at full N (TSE) ≈ 10× data-efficiency
TopBrain · cerebrovascular

LDM beats pix2pix on paired MRA→CTA

With dense paired-image conditioning (full MRA latent, not a binary mask), LDM substantially beats pix2pix: FID 95.7 vs 126.9, KID 0.031 vs 0.070. Opposite of the PASD pattern — conditioning density is what determines which architecture wins.

−24.6% FID vs pix2pix −55.9% KID +0.013 SSIM

The cross-dataset rule

The two applications inverted who wins — the deciding factor isn't the architecture, it's the conditioning density.

DimensionPASDTopBrain
Conditioning inputBinary placenta maskFull MRA latent (4-ch dense)
Conditioning density~10% of slice nonzero100% (whole image)

Takeaway. Sparse conditioning + small data favors deterministic L1 (pix2pix). Dense conditioning + paired data favors diffusion (LDM). Cross-modality pairing, when present, is a goldmine regardless of architecture.

TopBrain — four-way head-to-head

Held-out 3 test patients, N=200 axial slices. Plain LDM wins on FID/KID/LPIPS. Adding a vessel-mask conditioning channel (LDM combined) improves SSIM and PSNR but degrades distributional fidelity — a clean trade-off between paired pixel-match and distribution match.

The vessel-mask trade-off. Adding mask conditioning to LDM moves the model toward better paired pixel-match (SSIM +0.005, PSNR +0.26 dB) at the cost of worse distribution match (FID +9.4, KID +0.008). Likely because TopBrain's 41 vessel labels are very sparse (~1-2% of slice) — the extra 41 mostly-zero conditioning channels regularize texture toward something blander even as they help vessel-region placement. Implication: if downstream task cares about paired pixels (e.g. registration aid), use the combined model; if it cares about realism/distribution (e.g. augmentation), use plain LDM.
CycleGAN doesn't reproduce the PASD pattern on TopBrain. Cross-modality CycleGAN augmentation was the PASD-wide winning insight (TSTR Dice +7 pp at low data). On TopBrain it's mid-pack. Likely reasons: PASD's BTFE↔TSE are similar T-weighted MR sequences with slightly different patient sets, while TopBrain's CT↔MRA are radically different physical modalities from the same patient — leaving CycleGAN with less of an "unpaired data multiplier" effect. The cross-modality finding is data-dependent, not universal.

Above: LDM samples grid (4 cols: MRA · real CT · synthetic CT · diff×4). Diff column mostly dark = small per-pixel errors. (Pix2pix and CycleGAN grids in cv_eval/.)

SPADE vessel-mask → CTA — limited by data sparsity

SPADE trained to ep 60/100 then D-collapsed. Root cause isn't the model — TopBrain's 41 vessel labels cover only ~1-2% of each slice (vessels only, no skull/brain/CSF), so SPADE has near-zero conditioning signal for ~98% of the image.

Lesson. Mask-conditional generators fail gracefully on sparse-mask datasets; LDM with image-based conditioning is the right architecture when masks alone don't fill the image. (LDM's diffusion prior fills in unconditioned regions much better than SPADE's tanh head.)

★ Realism ≠ Relevance — cross-dataset utility study (TSTR)

The key finding. We measure not just whether synthetic images look real (FID) but whether they're useful: train a segmenter purely on each generator's synthetic data, test on real images (Train-on-Synthetic-Test-on-Real). Each value is mean ± std over 3 random seeds. The utility ranking does NOT match the image-quality ranking.

Synthetic again nearly matches real; LDM is robustly best/tied across all three datasets; the sparse-mask SPADE (~2% coverage) is worst — reinforcing the mask-density effect on a utility basis.

The headline: on both image→image datasets, the FID winner (plain LDM) is beaten on downstream utility by the combined LDM — which has worse FID. The combined model matches or slightly exceeds real data on prostate (0.731 vs 0.723) and reaches ~93% of real on TopBrain. With 3-seed error bars, every gap in the ranking (combined > LDM > pix2pix > CycleGAN) exceeds the std — the effect is robust, not noise. Picking a generator by FID/SSIM is the wrong criterion when the goal is downstream utility — you must measure TSTR. Confirmed across three datasets and two task types.

Augmentation — does real + synthetic beat real-only?

A complementary regime: train the segmenter on real data plus each generator's synthetic data (mean ± std over 3 seeds). Synthetic augmentation never materially hurts, and the generator that is worst synthetic-only (CycleGAN) becomes the best augmenter on prostate.

Augmentation rehabilitates weak generators. Where real data is the bottleneck (prostate), real+synthetic adds up to +1.4 pp Dice — and CycleGAN, the worst synthetic-only source (0.599), is the strongest augmenter (0.737). Where real-only is already strong (TopBrain), augmentation lands within ~1 pp (no harm). PASD gains are marginal-positive. The risk of adding synthetic data is low; the upside is largest exactly when you have little real data.

Prostate158 — three-way head-to-head (image quality)

Held-out 19 test patients, N=400 axial slices. LDM wins on FID/KID/LPIPS — and is the best FID we've achieved across all three datasets. Pix2pix and LDM tie on SSIM/PSNR (within noise). CycleGAN is worst on all 5 metrics — second confirmation that cross-modality CycleGAN doesn't generalize to physically-different modality pairs.

Best FID across all three datasets. Prostate LDM's FID of 72.7 beats TopBrain LDM (95.7) and PASD LDM (118.9). Likely because prostate has 4-5× more train patients (119 vs 25 TopBrain, vs ~25 PASD per modality) and the T2 conditioning carries strong anatomical structure that the LDM can exploit.
Cross-modality CycleGAN — exception, not rule. Second project after TopBrain where cross-modality CycleGAN underperforms on physically-different modality pairs. The PASD finding (CycleGAN BTFE↔TSE winning) now looks like a special case of same imaging type, slightly-different patient sets. CT↔MR or T2↔ADC pairs from the SAME patient give CycleGAN no "unpaired data multiplier" — pix2pix and LDM exploit the pairing CycleGAN throws away.

Prostate LDM vs published SOTA — honest standing

We benchmarked our T2→ADC LDM against the literature (deep research, 2026-06-09) and ran a no-retrain inference sweep + ROI-cropped eval. Two honest conclusions: our FID is competitive, our SSIM is genuinely behind the GAN+contrastive SOTA — and that gap is real, not a measurement artifact.

Ours LDM (CFG 1.0, single)0.5572.7default — best FID/realism
Ours LDM (CFG 1.0, ensemble×4)0.6288SSIM-priority option (in UI)

The SSIM gap is real. We hypothesised AI-ADC's 0.86 was inflated by ROI-cropping (background excluded). We tested it: cropping our output to the actual prostate-zone bounding box gives ROI-SSIM 0.54 ≈ our full-slice 0.55 — no inflation. The prostate ADC region is heterogeneous and genuinely hard to match. The remaining gap is some mix of their cleaner in-house data, their contrastive (PatchNCE) loss, and unverifiable SSIM normalization. We do not claim SSIM-SOTA.
What worked / didn't (this push). ✅ CFG 1.5→1.0 is a free strict win (better on every metric). ✅ Ensemble averaging is a real SSIM lever (0.55→0.62) at an FID cost — exposed as a UI knob. ❌ Adversarial latent diffusion (SynDiff-style LDM++) regressed the paired metrics — the adversarial gradient corrupted the diffusion backbone. The strong vanilla LDM remains our best model.

Prostate SPADE — sparsity hypothesis confirmed

SPADE anatomy_mask → T2 (separate task from the three above; mask-only conditioning, no image). Test set N=400. FID 294.7 vs LDM's 72.7 — much worse, but that's expected for a mask-only model. The actual finding is comparative: SPADE trains cleanly and produces believable surrounding anatomy on prostate, where it failed on TopBrain.

DatasetMask coverageSPADE result
TopBrain (vessels)~1-2 % of slice (vessel labels only)D-collapsed at ep 60/100; never produced plausible CTA
Prostate158 (anatomy)~30 % of slice (PZ + CG)D drifted at ep ~85, rolled back to ep 70; generates believable T2 with hallucinated bladder/pelvis
The conditioning-density threshold. Mask-conditional generators need a meaningful fraction of the image to carry condition. Below ~5 % coverage, SPADE has no signal to anchor texture and collapses. At ~30 % coverage (typical organ masks), SPADE generates plausible organs + plausibly-shaped surroundings. Practical rule: for mask→image translation, your mask should cover at least ~10-20 % of meaningful pixels — vessel-only or thin-structure masks need image-conditional methods like LDM instead.
